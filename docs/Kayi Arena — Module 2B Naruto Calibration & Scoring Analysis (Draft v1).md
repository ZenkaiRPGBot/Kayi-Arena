## 0. Status and verification statement (read first)

- **Rater:** Claude (sole rater). The independent AI review required by 2A §7.10 for Power 5, Fit 5, Low-confidence, exception and inference records has **not** been run. All 14 records are **Draft**.
- **Formula and weights preserved \[F\]:** Role Score = (Fit × Fit weight + Power × Power weight) ÷ 5. Weights (Fit/Power %): Captain 60/40, Strategist 85/15, Attacker 50/50, Tank 50/50, Assassin 65/35, Support 85/15, Healer 85/15. Equivalent: Captain 12F+8P, Strategist 17F+3P, Attacker 10F+10P, Tank 10F+10P, Assassin 13F+7P, Support 17F+3P, Healer 17F+3P. All scores below were calculated by this formula and re-added by hand. No ratings were adjusted for balance.
- **Reference verification: limited, and no record can be approved yet.** I did not open the manga itself. I could only locate a few arc and chapter ranges through secondary indexes (Wikipedia and Narutopedia, which are Tier 4 under 2A §7.2 and help find scenes but are not proof). Everything else is recalled from memory and is marked \[U\]. Under 2A §7.1, a reference recalled from memory cannot support an approved rating. I did not invent chapter numbers: where I could not locate one, only the arc or event is named.
  - **\[VI\] located via index, scene itself not read:** Part I is ch. 1–244 (Kakashi Gaiden ch. 239–244), Part II is ch. 245–700, 72 volumes. Sasuke vs Itachi is in vol. 43 (index lists ch. 390 'The Final Technique', 391 Kirin aftermath and Itachi's remark that Susanoo saved him, 392 'Susanoo', and 400 on the clan massacre). Kakashi vs Itachi is vol. 29 (ch. 254–262). Naruto vs Sasuke at the Valley of the End is ch. 218–229 (ch. 227 is Chidori vs Rasengan).
  - **\[U\] unverified:** everything else, including all Fourth War, Pain arc, Hidan/Kakuzu, Five Kage Summit and databook references.
- **Confidence convention in this draft:** the confidence shown is what I would assign if the cited scene is confirmed as I recall it. It is not a verification claim.
- **Grade shorthand:** A = scene shown in canon, B = capability-based inference or authoritative statement, C = weak. Confidence: H / M / L (definitions revised in Section 7).

## 1. Naruto canonical policy (Task 1)

This adds only Naruto-specific rules to 2A §7 and §8.2. General policy is unchanged.

| Topic | Naruto policy |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary medium (Tier 1)** | Masashi Kishimoto's Naruto manga, ch. 1–700, including the Kakashi Gaiden (ch. 239–244). Kishimoto-authored post-war manga Gaiden material counts as Tier 1 for post-war facts only. |
| **Tier 2** | Kishimoto-supervised databooks, Kishimoto interviews and statements, and official novels. Used for traits, background and stated abilities. They may clarify but never override Tier 1. Databook numeric stats are not imported as ratings and are at most Grade B statements. Novels cannot raise Power or Fit above what Tier 1 demonstrates. |
| **Tier 3** | Anime content that adapts manga events. Anime-original arcs, filler and anime-only fight extensions are excluded (2A §7.3 default). Animation spectacle is not evidence. Where anime and manga differ, the manga governs and the difference is logged. |
| **Tier 4 / not evidence** | Films, games, fan material, wikis (usable only to find a scene, never cited as proof). |
| **Naruto vs Boruto era** | The default ECV for every Naruto-era character is the Part II period (pre-war through the Fourth Shinobi World War). Boruto-era material (Boruto manga and anime, Two Blue Vortex) is treated as Tier 2 equivalent and only for post-war facts. Boruto-era states (for example Baryon Mode, Kurama's loss, lost eyes) form a separate version family and are chosen as ECV only if they beat the Part II prime under 2A §6.5, with ties going to Part II. Boruto-only abilities never enter a Part II ECV. No character in this sample uses a Boruto-era ECV. |
| **Versions and transformations** | Candidate version families: Part I, Part II pre-war, Fourth War, Boruto-era. ECV is the prime sustainable state (2A §6.2). Regularly accessible forms (Sage Mode, Kurama Mode, Eight Inner Gates, Byakugō) are part of the ECV, Conditional, and weighed by the five questions in 2A §6.3. Fatal or character-ending techniques (final Gate, Reaper Death Seal, Izanami-type self-sacrifice) are Excluded. |
| **Edo Tensei and revival** | A reanimated state is a separate candidate version. Abilities that come from the reanimation (immortality, unlimited chakra, no fatigue) are external and Excluded. The living version is the ECV for characters who were reanimated (Itachi, Minato). Skills and judgment shown while reanimated may support Fit as persisting traits (2A §6.6). A character who returns to life by technique and lives on (Madara by Rinne Tensei) is rated in that living state. |
| **Tailed beasts and Jinchūriki** | A host's access to a sealed beast's chakra is the host's own Conditional ability in the ECV while the beast is sealed in them and the access is repeatable. The beast acting as a separate agent is excluded. A character who has lost the beast in the ECV (Gaara after extraction) gets none of its abilities. Temporary control or possession by a third party is Excluded. |
| **Borrowed and granted abilities** | Power granted by another being is Excluded while borrowed (for example the Sage of Six Paths' gifts to Naruto). Permanently integrated acquisitions are included (transplanted Sharingan, Orochimaru's cells in Kabuto, a retained Rinnegan). **Decision flag N-1:** I excluded Naruto's Six Paths Sage Mode as a one-time grant. This is the most rating-sensitive policy call in the sample. See Section 3 and Section 6. |
| **Summons** | Contract summons (toads, slugs, snakes) are Conditional tools, weighed by 2A §6.3, and count for what the contract routinely provides (transport, communication, utility). A large named summon's own combat feats are not credited to the summoner. Edo Tensei armies count as separate characters and are excluded. |
| **Temporary vs permanent power-ups** | Permanent: eye evolutions, retained seals, completed training. Temporary: Gates, Sage Mode, Kurama Mode, Cursed Seal (Part I only), pills. Temporary forms are Conditional. One-time surges, assist-granted forms and desperation awakenings are Excluded. |

## 5. Draft v3 consistency fixes (Task 4)

These are clarifications. No approved decision is reopened.

### 5.1 Section 2: score range

Replace in the Section 2 introduction "every score is a whole number from 0 to 100" with: **"every score is a whole number between the role's minimum and 100. The minimums are Captain 8, Strategist 3, Attacker 10, Tank 10, Assassin 7, Support 3 and Healer 3, reached by Fit 0 with Power 1. A score of 0 is not attainable."** Section 2.1 already contains the minimums and is unchanged.

### 5.2 Section 6.3: multiple limitations on one ability

Replace the inclusion test with: **Classification depends on how the ability fails the four questions, not on how many it limits.**

- **Core:** all four answers are an unqualified yes.
- **Conditional:** the ability passes question 4 (it belongs to the version) and the character can use it by their own method (questions 1 and 3), but one or more of questions 1–3 carries a limit, such as time, cost, cooldown, terrain, preparation, a partner or risk. Any number of limits keeps it Conditional. Limits combine in the five-question weighing already in 6.3, so many limits lower the effect on the rating and never convert the ability to Excluded by count.
- **Excluded:** the answer to question 3 or 4 is a flat no (another being's ability, a one-off, outside the version), or the answer to question 1 is a flat no (the character cannot reuse it). Fatal or character-ending techniques are Excluded because they cannot be reused.
- **Partner-dependent abilities** are Conditional, not a failure of question 3, as 6.4 already states. A one-off helped event is Excluded.
- If limits are so severe that the ability reliably adds nothing, it stays Conditional and the weighing yields no uplift. No arbitrary exclusion is made.

### 5.3 Grade A direct evidence versus Grade B capability-based inference

Add to Section 7.4: **Grade A means the evidence is the capability or outcome itself shown in canon.** A reviewer can point to a scene where what the rating needs (for example durability against a named attack, successful infiltration, a plan that worked) appears with its effect. **Grade B means the rating needs a step beyond what is shown:** an authoritative statement, or an inference that a shown capability would suffice in a situation canon did not show. The grade attaches to the evidence for the level claimed, not to the scene. A rating can use both: the shown capability is A, and the added inference is B and sets the ceiling for the increment that depends on it (at most one inferential step, 7.6). Example: Susanoo withstanding a major attack is Grade A for durability. Concluding the same Susanoo could hold a line for teammates is Grade B. For Fit, 5.4's 'reliable canonical evidence of the capability the role needs' means the capability is shown (Grade A), so a role record is still not required.

### 5.4 Confidence definitions and reliable Grade B

Replace the table in 7.9 with:

| Confidence | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| High | At least two independent Grade A items, or one decisive and acknowledged Grade A item, with no unresolved conflict, and the level is not sensitive to a reasonable alternative reading. |
| Medium | One Grade A item; or Grade A plus Grade B; or **consistent, reliable Grade B** (premises are Grade A or authoritative, a stated mechanism, one inferential step, no contradiction). Minor ambiguity allowed. |
| Low | Grade C only; Grade B that is single-sourced, stretches beyond one inferential step, or has a plausible alternative level; unresolved conflicts; sparse evidence; adaptation-only support; or a level sensitive to the reading. |

This removes the earlier overlap in which 'Grade B only' appeared under both Medium and Low. A justified exception to a default ceiling (3.5, 5.4) still requires at least Medium.
